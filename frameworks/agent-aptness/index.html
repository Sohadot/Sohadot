Agent Aptness Framework — AAF 1.0

A task-specific framework for evaluating the correspondence between an agent and a defined assignment — turning a conceptual distinction into a documented, citable and applicable method.

Capability belongs to the agent. Aptness exists between the agent and the task.

The problem it addresses

The most capable agent can still be the wrong agent. Teams often select AI agents through broad capability impressions rather than documented task fit — yet the operative question is narrower: how well does this particular agent correspond to this particular task?

A broadly capable agent may be poorly matched to a specific assignment, while a narrower agent may be the stronger choice when its skills, knowledge, tools, operating environment and reliability evidence correspond more closely to the work. AAF makes that correspondence explicit and reviewable.

Capability vs. aptness

Capability is a general property of the agent — what it can do. Aptness is relational — it exists between a specific agent and a specific task, and it is conditional on the task's requirements, operating environment and evidence threshold.

Agent Aptness is the degree of correspondence between an agent's demonstrated characteristics and the stated requirements of a particular task.

The five dimensions

AAF 1.0 evaluates correspondence across five dimensions, each scored with a short evidence note.

S
Skill correspondence
Do the agent's demonstrated functional skills align with the operations the task requires?
K
Knowledge correspondence
Does the agent's domain knowledge align with the task's concepts, vocabulary and evidence?
T
Tool compatibility
Can the agent reliably use the tools, interfaces and formats the task requires?
E
Environment compatibility
Is the agent suited to the technical, temporal and operational environment of the work?
R
Reliability evidence
Is there repeatable, relevant evidence of performance on comparable assignments?

Fit classification — A0 to A5

Every assessment resolves to one legible class, not a loose capability impression.

A0
UnassessedEvidence is insufficient to determine task correspondence.
A1
MisfitOne or more essential requirements are absent, contradicted or incompatible.
A2
Limited FitCorresponds to part of the assignment but lacks material requirements.
A3
Conditional FitSuitable only under stated tools, constraints or support conditions.
A4
Operational FitCorresponds strongly within the defined operating environment.
A5
Demonstrated FitStrong, repeatable evidence across all essential dimensions.

Critical requirements are non-compensable

A task may designate one or more requirements as critical. A failed critical requirement cannot be compensated for by strength elsewhere: an agent that cannot use an essential tool should not receive a high fit class merely because it performs strongly across the other dimensions.
