only, solely, exclusively — and nothing beyond.

A genuine Scots-law adverb, most familiar from Scottish conveyancing (in liferent allenarly), where it marks the exact limit of a grant. Recorded in Scottish burgh records from 1703 — now developed into a live, cited reference page and available for acquisition.

adv. Scots Law /əˈlɛnərlɪ/ rec. 1703 Premium
The Word
A rare word that names a limit

Allenarly means only, solely, exclusively. In a Scottish deed it does one job: it marks the place where a grant stops.

The phrase in liferent allenarly conveys the right to enjoy a property for life and withholds every power beyond that — sale, encumbrance, bequest, and the fee itself. One word fences a right down to a single, exclusive scope.

Etymology: all + Scots anerly ("singly"), from ane ("one"). Variant spellings: allanerly, alainerly, alenarly, alinerly.

Not "exclusive"
Where the value sits

Exclusive — modern

Allenarly — Scots law

  • Faces inward.
  • Names what was never conveyed.
  • A limit on a right.
  • Rare outside Scots legal use. On record since 1703.
